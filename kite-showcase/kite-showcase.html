What we're imagining

  • The discount decides the format, not the template. Steps that add up on one measure (spend, or a count of items) can fill a bar. Several different steps become a checklist. A choice between steps becomes "pick one". No steps means the price just changes. A product-level reward can show on the price; an order-level one can't.
  • Kite explains the "no". A merchant who wants a progress bar for "add a serum, spend $60, be a VIP" is told why that would mislead the shopper, and what would make it fit (drop to one measure, or split it into tiers).
  • Hidden rules stay hidden. Customer tags, markets and campaign links decide who sees the offer. They never appear as a step, because the shopper can't act on them. Guests see "Log in to see member prices" where that applies.
  • One discount, many surfaces. The same discount can show as a bar in the cart, a price on the product page and a line in a custom bundle builder at the same time. All of them read the same live state, so they never disagree.
  • Kite stays responsible for the numbers. Whatever the surface, Kite makes sure the price it shows is the price at checkout. That's what makes route 3 safe to hand to a merchant's developer or AI.

Questions to settle

  • Where does this screen live? A "Show on store" step at the end of creating any discount, a single storefront page across all discounts, or both?
  • Several discounts on one surface. Free shipping at $50 and a gift at $100 share one measure, so one bar can carry both. A spend goal and an item goal can't share a bar. Should Kite suggest combinations across discounts?
  • How much freedom in route 3? Live values only, or also Kite's own building blocks (bar, checklist, price) that a merchant's design can drop in?
  • Unlock prices before the shopper qualifies. "$34 when you buy 3" sells the offer, but it isn't the price yet. Is that always shown, or a merchant choice?
